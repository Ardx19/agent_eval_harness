**What the engine actually returned.** Every one of the 10 routes is an **achiral Knoevenagel/conjugate-addition disconnection**:

A planar electrophile attacked from either face gives the two enantiomers in equal amounts. So `CC(=O)CC(c1ccccc1)*` is produced as a **racemate** — identical to the commercial product.

**The "enantiomeric excess it will give" is 0% ee.** The reported `policy_probability` (0.89 for the top route) is a *model confidence in the bond disconnection*, not a yield or a stereo outcome, and `metadata.stereochemistry_inverted` is a canned flag — there's no stereochemical logic behind it. ## How to actually get (S)-warfarin

The chemistry the model can't do is the stereocontrol step, so you must bolt it on:

1. **Asymmetric conjugate addition (best).** Coumarin C3 + benzylideneacetone using a chiral phase-transfer or bifunctional-cinchona/H-bond catalyst on the enolate, or a chiral Lewis-acid activating the enone. (S)-selective conditions for exactly this 4-hydroxycoumarin + benzylideneacetone system are well precedented in the literature; ee is catalyst/condition-dependent (typically 80–95% ee achievable, needing a quick screen), *not* something the route JSON can tell you.
2. **Chiral auxiliary / Evans or Oppolzer type** on the enolate or enone for a diastereoselective addition, then cleave — reliable, higher ee (usually >95% de) at the cost of two extra steps.
3. **Classical resolution** — the commercial racemate route plus chiral amine (e.g. quinine/α-phenylethylamine) salt or chiral chromatography. This gives (S)-warfarin but caps at ~50% of the racemate, not a direct ee.

**Bottom line:** the retrosynthesis gives a clean, stock-feasible 4-step racemic skeleton (top route: benzaldehyde + acetone + 4-hydroxycoumarin, all in stock, ~2 steps to the key Michael acceptor), but the enantioselective step must be the asymmetric conjugate addition — without it the ee is **0%**, not the implied high ee.
